## Carbon credits: a primer

Carbon credits are tradable instruments that represent the avoided or enhanced removal of one metric ton of CO2 or its equivalent, used for offsetting or compliance within carbon markets (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offsets_and_credits). They are generated through mitigation projects like reforestation or renewable energy, following specific methodologies and standards such as CERs or VERs (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offsets_and_credits). The markets operate via mechanisms like cap-and-trade schemes, where allowances are limited and traded to meet emission reduction targets, with the European Union Emissions Trading System being the largest (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emission_trading). Criticisms include concerns over quality, overestimation, double-counting, and greenwashing, leading to efforts for better credit quality assessment (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_offsets_and_credits).
